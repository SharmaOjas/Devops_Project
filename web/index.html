How it works

From a radio ping to a reasoned answer

Four steps run in the background, every minute, whether anyone is watching or not.

‹
›
Every 60 secondsAll or nothingNeighbours checkedBoth dots shown

1 · Collect

A background program asks the open data sources for ship positions, ship details, sea state, wind and waves every minute. If one fails, it tries again.

2 · Validate

New records wait in a holding area. The database then checks each one and keeps it or refuses it (repeats, impossible positions, impossible jumps) and writes down why.

3 · Detect

Ships that stay quiet for 20 minutes are checked. Are they in port? Parked? Leaving our area? Did the ships around them go quiet too? Only the unexplained ones remain.

4 · Reveal

Both positions, plus the sea area the ship could have crossed, with land removed and the speed adjusted for wind and waves. It shows what was possible, not what happened.

Help: how to use every feature

On every page
  • The How it works page walks you through the data step by step, shows where it comes from, checks it against the official source and explains every database table.
  • Boxes with an arrow open and close when you click their title.
  • Hover a number in the strip under the header to see what it means. The text at the top of each page says what the page is for.
Live Map
  • Click a ship to see where it has been, its speed, its destination history and any times it went silent. Replay plays its journey; the slider moves through it.
  • Search box (left): find a ship by name or ID.
  • Click a red ship or a row under “Ships going dark” to see its last position, where it came back, the sea area it could have crossed, and a checklist explaining why it counts as going dark.
  • Layers (top right): switch things on or off, such as wind, waves, ports and busy sea lanes. Click “Layers” to fold the box away.
  • Click empty water to clear your choice.
Going Dark
  • The slider sets how long a ship must be quiet before it counts as silent. The bars show how many silent ships have a normal reason and how many do not.
  • “Silent and unexplained” lists the rest. Click one to open it on the map. “History of silences” lists every case, with filters.
Pipeline
  • The picture shows data moving from the sources, through the check, into storage. The small cards show how each data source is doing. The last box lists refused records and why.
Temporal
  • Pick a ship. The top bars show when each destination was true. The diamonds underneath show when our system learned about it.
  • “Look back in time” shows what the destination was at a moment you choose.
Ports
  • Each card shows how many ships are in a port. The list shows arrivals and departures.
Database health and safety
  • The buttons at the top switch between Health, Permissions, Two at once, Speed and Backup.
  • Permissions: “Test the permissions” tries things each user should and should not be able to do.
  • Two at once: shows what happens when several people change the same ship together.
  • Speed: do steps 1, 2, 3 and then 2 again to see how much an index speeds up a search.
  • Backup: saves a copy of the database and proves it can be restored.
Demo controls
  • Load demo ships adds made-up ships so you can see every situation. Remove the demo ships deletes them. The other buttons refresh live data or redo the checks.
  • Click the logo to go back to the opening page. Press Esc to close this help.
